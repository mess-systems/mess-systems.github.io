The mess.systems style grows from the same idea as the project. Russell Ackoff called a system of interconnected problems a *mess*. The style shows one movement: from a tangle of links to a system you can read. The story of how it came about is in a [post in Russian](/ru/blog/style-from-mess/).

## The mark

The m is drawn with a single line. Its two arches are two feedback loops. The dot in the name mess.systems sits on the boundary: mess on the left, systems on the right. It is the only coloured element of the logo.

- Clear space: at least half the height of the mark on every side.
- The mark from 16 px, the wordmark from 96 px wide. Below 24 px, the mark only.
- Don't stretch, rotate, recolour the dot or add effects.

## Colour

Graphite and paper are the base; one signal red is the accent.

- Graphite `#1F2328`: text and the mark.
- Paper `#FAF9F6`: background.
- Signal `#E5482D`: the dot and whatever needs attention. One signal element per screen.
- Links `#0B5CAD`, neutrals `#59636E` and `#D8DEE4`: the same as on the current site.

Every colour has a light and a dark variant; the text pairs pass WCAG AA, and the pure red is kept for graphics and large text.

## Type

- [IBM Plex Sans](https://github.com/IBM/plex) for text and headings.
- [IBM Plex Mono](https://github.com/IBM/plex) for the wordmark, labels and code.
- [Noto Sans SC](https://github.com/notofonts/noto-cjk) for Chinese.

All three are open under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## Graphics

Nodes and links: a regular grid, disorder on the left fading out by the middle, one or two feedback loops on the right and a single signal node on the boundary. Diagrams in the spirit of Stafford Beer's Viable System Model. An 8 px grid, no gradients, no stock photos.
