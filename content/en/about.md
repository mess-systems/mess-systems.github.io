## The idea

Most real problems do not come alone. Russell Ackoff called a set of interdependent, changing problems a *mess*: a system of problems that each stakeholder sees differently. In a mess, fixing one part on its own can make the whole worse. Ackoff's answer was to plan together with the people involved and to redesign the system so the problem dissolves, rather than to keep treating symptoms.

The move from the pre-AI era to the AI era is that kind of mess. Work, companies, people and systems are changing at the same time, and they change each other. New kinds of systems appear, and old assumptions about roles, tools and decisions stop holding. mess.systems is an open project that works through this shift in public.

To go deeper into Ackoff's thinking:

- [Russell L. Ackoff, "The Future of Operational Research is Past"](https://doi.org/10.1057/jors.1979.22), Journal of the Operational Research Society, 1979, the classic source of the term
- [Systems Thinking Alliance overview of Russell Ackoff](https://systemsthinkingalliance.org/russell-ackoff/)

## What you will find here

mess.systems uses one organization, also called mess.systems, as a worked example. Instead of abstract advice, you see real artifacts: the standards it sets, the technologies it chooses, the decisions it makes and the reasoning behind them. Everything is published openly so you can reuse it, adapt it or challenge it.

Expect:

- **Standards and templates** you can copy into your own organization.
- **Decisions with reasons**, including what was rejected and why.
- **Notes on change**: how work, roles and systems shift once AI is part of everyday operations.

Current projects are listed on the [projects page](/projects/). Both are licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

## Who is behind it

mess.systems is maintained by Pavel Evdokimov.

Pavel works where technology, organizations and people meet. He looks at a company as a whole system and focuses on the changes that improve the whole, not only one of its parts. In his own words:

> For many years I worked with enterprise content management systems for large organizations. Now I lead AI adoption across the whole enterprise: from strategy for product, engineering and the office to a shared platform for AI agents with identity, secrets, AI gateways and sandboxes. Models change, but the harness around them remains the company's investment. That is what my talks, courses and the material here are about.
