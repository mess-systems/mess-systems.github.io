Most organizations keep a list of problems: delivery is slow, costs are high, security findings keep coming back, people are tired of new tools. Each item gets an owner and a target. Then the list grows anyway.

Russell Ackoff had a word for this. In [The Future of Operational Research is Past](https://doi.org/10.1057/jors.1979.22) (1979) he wrote that managers do not face independent problems. They face dynamic situations made of changing problems that interact with each other, and he called those situations *messes*. A mess is a system of problems.

## What changes when you see a mess

If problems are connected, three things follow.

First, you cannot fix them one at a time. A change in one place shifts the conditions everywhere else, sometimes in the opposite direction from what you wanted.

Second, there is no single correct description. The same situation looks like a risk to one group, a delay to another and an opportunity to a third. All of them are partly right.

Third, local improvement is not the same as improvement. Every department can hit its own targets while the whole organization gets worse.

Ackoff's way out was not a better fix for each symptom. He argued for planning at the level of the whole system, with the people who live in it, and for *dissolving* problems: redesigning the system or its environment so the problem stops arising in its current form.

## The AI shift is a mess

The move from the pre-AI era to the AI era fits this description closely. Work changes, because tasks that took a team now take a person and a set of agents. Companies change, because decisions, controls and responsibilities need new homes. People change, because roles, skills and careers are being redrawn. Systems change, because software now has to identify, authorize and observe agents as well as humans.

None of these can be handled on its own. A new tool changes roles. New roles change controls. New controls change what tools are allowed. It is a loop, not a list.

## What mess.systems is for

mess.systems is an open organization used as a worked example of this shift. Instead of general advice, it publishes concrete artifacts with the reasoning behind them:

- a [naming standard](https://github.com/mess-systems/naming-standard) that gives people, services and AI agents one shared grammar for names;
- a [tech radar](https://github.com/mess-systems/tech-radar) that records which technologies the organization uses, how firmly and why.

More will follow as the organization grows: decisions, templates and notes on what worked and what did not. Everything is licensed under CC BY 4.0, so you can take it, adapt it and tell us where it is wrong.
